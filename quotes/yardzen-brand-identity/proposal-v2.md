## The brand is not held to the standard the work is

Your brief says it plainly:

> "Erosion of the brand equity due to a lack of adherence to standards: inconsistent font usage, varying tones of voice, lack of brand breakthrough, and brand visuals that lack the quality standards expected."

Yardzen delivers world-class landscape design every week. The brand around it does not present at that level, and you have already connected that gap to revenue: **more brand quality means more leads and more design purchases**.

---

## Nobody is ignoring the brand. There is nothing to follow

Every marketing request today is answered from scratch. Someone picks a typeface, chooses a crop, guesses a colour.

Some of it lands on brand. Some of it does not. All of it costs a designer.

**The standards slip because doing it right is slower than doing it wrong.** That is a system problem, not a discipline problem, and it does not improve on its own.

## What we are actually building

Not a logo. Not a PDF that gets saved and never opened.

**A system that makes the on-brand version the easy version**, so the people asking for work stop needing us in the room to get it right.

Three things make that true: a mark with enough character to be worth using, a compositional system that organises every layout, and rules written for someone who was not in the room.

## Phase 1 · Foundations

**What does Yardzen look like.**

- Logo architecture. The full system of marks and how they relate
- The symbol. We explore **YZ** in multiple directions, and evaluate the current **Y in the circle** standalone and beside the wordmark. It is designed to work on a hat as well as a header
- Lockups, safe areas, minimum sizes
- Colour system. HEX, RGB, CMYK, Pantone, and the rule for deriving new accents so the palette extends without drifting
- Typography. Arsenal, Geist, and one further open-licence face
- Graphic elements, iconography, art direction
- **The compositional system.** The grid, headline zones, CTA placement. This is the part that makes it a system instead of a style sheet

**First logo directions in front of you September 29.**

---

## Phase 2 · Copy

**What are the rules, in writing.**

A system nobody can read is a system nobody can follow. This phase is the book itself.

- Visual principles. Photography, AI-generated artwork, the role of humanity, video, angles and cropping
- Dos and don'ts, written for a contractor, a partner, or a marketing hire on their first week
- **Sub-brand derivation rule.** How any future sub-brand takes its colour and lockup from the master brand without a new design project each time
- **Co-branding rules.** How Yardzen sits beside a partner's mark, so partner work stops being negotiated case by case

The book ships the rule. Named sub-brands get derived from it later, which is Q1 work.

## We deliberately do not repaint the product yet

An unreleased brand should not be applied to live product UI.

Through the build we align the **structure** of the design tokens to the incoming brand and flag conflicts. We do not restyle the product against a system still under review, because applying it early means doing it twice.

That adoption is what Q1 is for. It lands in the product once it is approved, not while it is being decided.

## How we work

**A weekly brand review, separate from the product meeting.** Brand decisions need brand deciders in the room, and a review sharing product's agenda is a review that gets postponed. On a fixed schedule that turns into a missed date.

**If product scope grows during the build, it carries to Q1.** It does not get absorbed alongside a fixed-date deliverable.

**Two revision rounds per phase.** A round is one consolidated set of feedback from the approver, not opinions arriving separately.
