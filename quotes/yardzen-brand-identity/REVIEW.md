# Five review passes

Run 11 Sep 2026 against the three variants, each pass reading as somebody different.

---

## Pass 1 · Brian Radics, the buyer

He wrote five objectives in the brief. **The proposal does not answer them anywhere.**
The earlier draft did. Dropping them is a regression: a buyer who wrote a numbered
list looks for his numbers coming back. FIXED — objectives page added, each mapped
to the deliverable that answers it.

He has the Topgolf book as his quality reference, ~24 spec'd artifacts across 8
formats. We ship 8. That is stated, but in fine print under the table. FIXED —
promoted to its own bolded line.

---

## Pass 2 · A CFO who has never met us

Reads the money table fine. Then asks the question nobody wrote an answer to:
**what happens if we stop.** A four-month fixed-fee engagement with no exit
language is the kind of thing procurement sends back. FIXED — one termination line
in Terms. Deliberately short, because a long one invites negotiation.

Also asked and already answered: IP transfer, payment timing, currency, whether
product spend is at risk.

---

## Pass 3 · A skeptical creative director

Found the real hole. **Phase 1 delivers "a brand guide" and Phase 2 delivers
"the book".** A reader lands on that and asks why they are paying $21,000 more for
a document they were handed in September. It reads like the same deliverable sold
twice, and it is the single thing most likely to stall approval.

The distinction is real, it just was not written down:

- **September is the brand.** It shows what Yardzen looks like, and it proves it by
  being set in the identity itself.
- **November is the rules.** It tells a stranger how to use it without a briefing.

FIXED, and made load-bearing: the phases are now titled *the brand · the rules ·
the templates*, and the September page says outright that the guide shows the brand
but does not yet tell anyone how to use it.

---

## Pass 4 · Dan, reading it out loud

Every header is sayable as a sentence. That holds.

13 pages is long to walk through live. B is 11 and moves faster. Left as is, because
A and C are built to be read, B is built to be presented, and that is the actual
difference between them.

## Pass 6 · Against Yardzen's own Tone and Voice Guide

Dan shared it on 11 Sep. It is the Brand Foundation half of the book, it already
exists, and it is genuinely good. Saved here as `yardzen-tone-and-voice.md`.

Audited the proposal against its rules:

| Their rule | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| **Contractions always** | **0 in the whole document**, 45+ uncontracted forms | 38 |
| Numbers over adjectives | Already holds: 197 call sites, 8 surfaces, 15 slides | unchanged |
| Aspirational words once per piece, with a concrete data point | 2, both quoting Brian's own objectives | unchanged |
| Exclamation points earned | 1 | unchanged |
| Declaratives, "we say what's true" | Headers are already claims, not labels | unchanged |
| No hard sell, no manufactured urgency, no brag | Holds | unchanged |
| End with a clear next step, owned, with a date | **Missing.** It ended on Q1. | Added: who does what, by when |

The contractions line is the one that matters, and it is not a style preference.
Their guide says writing without them **"reads like a terms-of-service page"** —
and Dan's verdict on the previous draft was that it *"reads like a contract."*
Same diagnosis, arrived at twice independently. Fixed.

Also changed on the strength of the guide: Phase 2 now says outright that half
of it already exists. The voice guide does not get rewritten, it gets a face,
and the visual rules get written to sit beside it at the same standard. That is
true, it de-risks the phase, and it shows we read what they sent.

**One judgment call for Dan:** this applies Yardzen's voice rules to a Casa Soda
document. It reads native to them and proves we absorbed the guide. If you would
rather the proposal sound like Soda than like Yardzen, the contractions are the
thing to reverse.
